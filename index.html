What prompted this

On May 1, 2026, the Nasdaq-100 Index adopted a new "fast-entry" rule. Under it, a large-cap company with a low public float that lists on Nasdaq can be evaluated for index inclusion beginning around the 7th trading day after its IPO — and added around the 15th trading day. That's far faster than the old process.

Several large private companies — most prominently SpaceX — have been reported to be considering Nasdaq listings in 2026. If one qualifies under the fast-entry rule, it would appear in funds like QQQ and QQQM within weeks of its IPO.

The S&P 500 indexes have not adopted a similar rule. They still require 12 months of trading history and GAAP profitability. So which funds are affected — and when — depends entirely on which index they track.

This site helps you understand those distinctions. It does not tell you what to do about them. Read the full explainer →
